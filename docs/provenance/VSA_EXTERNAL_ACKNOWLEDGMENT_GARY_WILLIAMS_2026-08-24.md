## Source chronology

In a public LinkedIn discussion, Taiwo M. Yusuf introduced a Validation Standing distinction between:

- current action permission; and
- current supportability of a regulated system for its defined intended use.

The comment further stated that a material model, dependency, configuration, evidence, or operating-condition change can require reassessment of current Validation Standing even when no action is pending, while historical validation remains intact.

Gary Williams then replied "agreed" and explicitly reformulated the distinction as:

`SYSTEM VALIDATION STANDING != ACTION PERMISSION`

He further separated:

`HISTORICAL SYSTEM VALIDATION != CURRENT SYSTEM STANDING != CURRENT ACTION PERMISSION`

and described two separate reassessment surfaces:

- system-level reassessment when the basis supporting intended use materially changes; and
- action-level revalidation immediately before consequence.

His reply also stated that neither reassessment surface should silently substitute for the other and that valid authority to act cannot manufacture the current regulated basis for trusting the system performing the action.

## Provenance interpretation

This record supports the bounded proposition that an external governance architect publicly recognized the separability of system-level Validation Standing and action-level permission after the VSA distinction was explicitly presented in the discussion.

This is useful external acknowledgment of the conceptual boundary:

`HISTORICAL_VALIDATION != CURRENT_VALIDATION_STANDING != ACTION_ADMISSIBILITY_OR_PERMISSION`

It is not evidence that Elias implements VSA, adopts COBIT-Chain, or is architecturally equivalent to COBIT-Chain.

It is also not evidence of copying, ownership transfer, priority over independently developed concepts, certification, endorsement, partnership, or independent reproduction of VSA.
