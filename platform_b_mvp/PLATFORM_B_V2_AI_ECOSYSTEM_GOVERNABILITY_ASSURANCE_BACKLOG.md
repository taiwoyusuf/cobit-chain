## Candidate capability

AI Ecosystem Governability Assurance

## Purpose

AI Ecosystem Governability Assurance verifies whether the full digital ecosystem supporting AI-enabled outputs, decisions, workflows, agents, evidence, and execution remains governable, traceable, secure, auditable, explainable, and regulator-defensible.

## v2 question

Can the organization demonstrate continuous trust in the ecosystem that allowed AI-supported output or action to become relied upon?

## Candidate concepts

### AI-Supported Output Ecosystem Assurance

Verifies the data, metadata, systems, records, human review, traceability, and lifecycle controls supporting an AI-generated or AI-assisted output.

### Digital Ecosystem Inspection Readiness

Prepares the organization to show regulators not only the model, but the surrounding evidence ecosystem: source data, metadata, audit trails, governance decisions, human oversight, lifecycle control, and explainability.

### Compliance-to-Capability Bridge

Connects system-level regulatory compliance with organizational AI governance capability.

The AI Act asks whether a system complies.

ISO 42001 asks whether the organization can govern AI repeatedly at scale.

### Organizational AI Maturity Evidence Trail

Shows whether AI governance is repeatable across many systems, not manually reinvented for every use case.

### Execution Governability Assurance

Verifies whether the AI-enabled execution system remains influenceable, correctable, stoppable, and accountable as conditions change.

### Operational Influence Continuity

Measures whether leadership and accountable owners still have structured ability to redirect, constrain, escalate, revoke, or stop AI-enabled execution.

### Governability Drift Signal

Detects when execution still appears to be working, but authority, context, ownership, assumptions, dependencies, or intervention ability are weakening.

### Zero Trust AI Evidence Fabric

Links identity, least privilege, policy enforcement, tool governance, RAG security, memory security, supply chain provenance, runtime monitoring, and continuous validation into evidence.

### AI Supply Chain Bill-of-Materials Assurance

Tracks model, dataset, agent, prompt, tool, adapter, plugin, and workflow components through MBOM, DBOM, ABOM, and PBOM-style evidence.

### Dynamic Agent Trust Evidence

Captures changing trust signals around agent behavior, tool usage, policy violations, telemetry, context risk, authorization decisions, and containment actions.

## Protected doctrine

Regulators may not inspect the AI alone. They may inspect the ecosystem that made the AI believable.

## Stronger doctrine

A model can be valid while the ecosystem around it is not governable.

## Platform B positioning

Platform B should not compete with FDA, EMA, ISO 42001, ServiceNow, Microsoft, Zero Trust platforms, or AI security tools.

Platform B should sit above and across them as:

The assurance layer that proves the AI ecosystem remained governable from data to evidence to decision to execution to regulatory defense.
