## Candidate capability

Platform B v2 Candidate — Regulatory-Grade AI Evidence Assurance

## Purpose

Regulatory-Grade AI Evidence Assurance verifies whether AI-generated or AI-assisted evidence is scientifically valid, reproducible, transparent, traceable, records-governed, human-reviewed, and defensible before regulators.

The assurance concern is not only whether an AI system generated useful evidence.

The assurance concern is whether the regulated organization can defend the AI-generated or AI-assisted evidence across the medicinal product lifecycle.

## v2 question

Can this AI-generated evidence be defended before a regulator across the medicinal product lifecycle?

## v2 candidate concepts

### 1. Regulatory-Grade AI Evidence Assurance

Verifies whether AI-assisted evidence is scientifically valid, reproducible, transparent, traceable, and acceptable for regulatory reliance.

Candidate assurance dimensions include:

- scientific validity
- reproducibility
- transparency
- traceability
- context of use
- human review
- data lineage
- model or tool version
- evidence source
- audit trail
- record classification
- lifecycle continuity

### 2. AI Evidence Lifecycle Continuity

Tracks AI-generated evidence across the medicinal product lifecycle, including:

- discovery
- clinical development
- CMC
- manufacturing
- regulatory submission
- pharmacovigilance
- post-market surveillance

This ensures AI evidence is not treated as a disconnected project artifact.

### 3. AI Records Lifecycle Assurance

Classifies AI prompts, outputs, logs, model versions, inference records, human reviews, audit trails, and evidence packages as managed records with retention and disposition rules.

Candidate record objects include:

- prompts
- outputs
- retrieval records
- source references
- model versions
- agent versions
- tool-call logs
- inference records
- evaluation records
- human review records
- approval records
- audit trails
- evidence packets

### 4. AI Artifact Retention and Disposition Assurance

Ensures every AI artifact has a defined:

- retention period
- disposition rule
- metadata requirement
- legal hold status
- lifecycle owner
- record classification
- reconstruction requirement
- auditability requirement

This prevents AI-generated work from becoming unmanaged operational residue.

### 5. Unified Information Governance-to-AI Assurance Bridge

Maps privacy, records management, cybersecurity, legal/compliance, risk, transparency, training, and culture into one AI evidence foundation.

This bridge prevents AI assurance from being separated from enterprise information governance.

### 6. Regulator-Ready AI Assurance Packet

Produces a structured package showing:

- context of use
- data lineage
- model / tool version
- human oversight
- validation evidence
- reproducibility evidence
- record classification
- retention rule
- audit trail
- limitations
- accountable owner
- lifecycle stage

### 7. Scientific Validity-to-Operational Trust Bridge

Links scientific evidence validity with operational trust.

This shows not only that the AI method worked, but that the surrounding workflow, records, review, and evidence controls are defensible.

### 8. Lifecycle-Wide AI Governance Assurance

Ensures AI governance is not designed project by project, but across the entire medicinal product lifecycle.

Candidate lifecycle coverage includes:

- research
- development
- clinical
- CMC
- manufacturing
- quality
- regulatory
- safety
- post-market monitoring

### 9. AI Activity and Governance Coverage Matrix

Maps AI use, AI development, agent development, shadow AI, evals, observability, trustworthiness, and agent lifecycle management against governance coverage gaps.

This concept recognizes that many governance approaches fail when they do not distinguish:

- governance of AI use
- governance of AI development
- governance of agent development
- governance of AI outputs
- governance of AI records
- governance of AI evidence
- governance of AI-assisted decisions
- governance of agent lifecycle management

### 10. Regulatory Credibility Score

Measures whether AI evidence can withstand regulator review based on:

- validity
- reproducibility
- traceability
- human oversight
- records governance
- auditability
- lifecycle continuity
- source authority
- context fit
- limitation disclosure
- reconstruction ability
