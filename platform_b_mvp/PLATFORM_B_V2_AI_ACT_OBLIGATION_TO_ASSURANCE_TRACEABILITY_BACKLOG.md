## Candidate capability

AI Act Obligation-to-Assurance Traceability

## Purpose

AI Act Obligation-to-Assurance Traceability maps EU AI Act risk categories, provider obligations, deployer obligations, transparency requirements, human oversight, logging, documentation, data governance, post-market monitoring, and incident reporting into operational evidence that can be continuously demonstrated.

## v2 question

Can this organization prove that each applicable AI Act obligation is satisfied through evidence, controls, ownership, monitoring, and review?

## Candidate concepts

### AI Act Risk Tier Assurance

Classifies AI systems as unacceptable, high-risk, transparency-risk, or minimal/no-risk and links each classification to required controls and evidence.

### Prohibited Use Boundary Assurance

Detects and prevents AI use cases that fall into prohibited or unacceptable-risk categories.

### High-Risk AI Evidence Passport

Captures the evidence needed for high-risk AI: risk management, data governance, logging, documentation, human oversight, robustness, cybersecurity, accuracy, and monitoring.

### Provider-to-Deployer Evidence Chain

Tracks what the provider must supply and what the deployer must monitor, review, retain, and evidence during operational use.

### Human Oversight Evidence Assurance

Proves that human oversight is not symbolic.

The reviewer must have authority, competence, context, escalation ability, and evidence of intervention.

### AI Activity Logging Assurance

Verifies that activity logs are retained, traceable, linked to decisions/actions, and reviewable for audit or regulator inspection.

### Transparency Obligation Assurance

Ensures users are informed when interacting with AI, and that AI-generated content is labelled or identified where required.

### GPAI Dependency Assurance

Tracks whether a downstream AI system depends on a general-purpose AI model and what evidence must be inherited from the GPAI provider.

### Post-Market / Post-Deployment Monitoring Assurance

Connects ongoing monitoring, incidents, malfunctions, complaints, drift, and corrective actions to the evidence record.

### Regulatory Timeline Readiness Signal

Tracks when obligations become applicable and whether the organization has evidence readiness before enforcement dates.

## Protected doctrine

The AI Act creates obligations. Platform B converts obligations into operational evidence.

## Stronger doctrine

Compliance is not a document. Compliance is the ability to prove that required controls worked when AI was used.

## Platform B positioning

Platform B should not compete with the EU AI Act, regulators, ISO 42001, legal teams, GRC tools, or compliance platforms.

Platform B should sit above and across them as:

The assurance layer that converts AI regulatory obligations into evidence-backed operational trust.
